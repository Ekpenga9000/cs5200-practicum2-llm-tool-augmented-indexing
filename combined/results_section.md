# Results

Seven benchmarks had complete, aligned baseline, Condition A, and Condition B timing files. JOB was excluded because its Condition B data does not exist. The high-confidence findings that are safe to lead with are TPC-H, TPC-DS, and SSB: their file provenance and timing checks do not show the known stale-baseline signature. TPC-H is the clearest positive case: Condition B improved over baseline by 96.724%, versus 88.512% for Condition A, for an 8.212 percentage-point benefit. SSB is the clearest negative case: B regressed on 8/13 queries and produced a -55.911 percentage-point benefit relative to A.

Condition A was stronger for TPC-DS, STATS, TPC-C, SSB, and DSB. TPC-DS is a high-confidence negative result because its baseline and Condition B files were corrected together; B improved 7.146% versus A's 23.229%. The largest gap was SSB, where B was 19.136% slower than baseline and 55.911 percentage points worse than A. STATS, DSB, and especially TPC-C should be presented with hedging: STATS is outlier-sensitive, DSB's mean is strongly influenced by Q37 and Q48, and TPC-C has an unresolved input-provenance gap. TPC-C's -2.206 percentage-point result is low-confidence and should not be used as headline evidence that tool access hurt performance.

At query level, the clearest B wins were TPC-H Q11, Q7, and Q10, where B recovered from large A regressions and improved 76.6%, 38.4%, and 59.3%, respectively. TATP Q8 also favored B (46.7% improvement versus 13.3% for A), but TATP's overall +4.569 pp result is medium-confidence because Q10/Q12 account for 44.5% of baseline time and cache state is unrecorded. Conversely, SSB Q4.3 and Q2.1 and DSB Q44 show substantial B regressions after A improvements. Some cases were nearly ties, such as SSB Q2.3 and Q1.3.

TPC-C requires especially cautious interpretation. Q12 accounts for nearly all of its 16.559 ms baseline mean; the other 11 queries are at most 0.252 ms. Its summary reports standard one-warehouse scale and clean median-of-7 measurements, making the small point-lookup timings plausible, but the current canonical schema/workload files postdate the Aug 4 results. The -2.206 percentage-point B benefit relative to A should therefore not be promoted as a headline finding until input identity is confirmed.

These comparisons should be read with the data caveats in `open_questions.md`: JOB is excluded because Condition B is missing, historical timeout policy is not uniformly encoded, and Git chronology cannot by itself prove identical database snapshots for every older benchmark.
