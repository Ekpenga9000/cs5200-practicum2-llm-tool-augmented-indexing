# Results

Eight benchmarks now have complete, aligned baseline, Condition A, and Condition B timing files. The high-confidence findings that are safe to lead with are TPC-H, TPC-DS, and SSB: their file provenance and timing checks do not show the known stale-baseline signature. TPC-H is the clearest positive case: Condition B improved over baseline by 96.724%, versus 88.512% for Condition A, for an 8.212 percentage-point benefit. SSB is the clearest negative case: B regressed on 8/13 queries and produced a -55.911 percentage-point benefit relative to A. JOB adds a strongly negative, medium-confidence result: B improved 49/113 queries but its aggregate was -317.782% versus baseline, compared with A's 72.684% improvement.

Condition A was stronger for TPC-DS, STATS, TPC-C, SSB, DSB, and JOB. TPC-DS is a high-confidence negative result because its baseline and Condition B files were corrected together; B improved 7.146% versus A's 23.229%. The largest gap was SSB, where B was 19.136% slower than baseline and 55.911 percentage points worse than A. STATS and DSB should still be presented with hedging because STATS is outlier-sensitive and DSB's mean is strongly influenced by Q37 and Q48. TPC-C's -2.206 percentage-point result has high confidence for input/result provenance, while JOB's -390.466 percentage-point Tool Benefit is medium-confidence because its clean redo and same-commit file set reduce stale-baseline risk but its mean is dominated by query 28a.

At query level, the clearest B wins were TPC-H Q11, Q7, and Q10, where B recovered from large A regressions and improved 76.6%, 38.4%, and 59.3%, respectively. TATP Q8 also favored B (46.7% improvement versus 13.3% for A), but TATP's overall +4.569 pp result is medium-confidence because Q10/Q12 account for 44.5% of baseline time and cache state is unrecorded. Conversely, SSB Q4.3 and Q2.1 and DSB Q44 show substantial B regressions after A improvements. Some cases were nearly ties, such as SSB Q2.3 and Q1.3.

TPC-C remains outlier-sensitive but no longer has an input-provenance gap. Q12 accounts for nearly all of its 16.559 ms baseline mean; the other 11 queries are at most 0.252 ms. Its summary reports standard one-warehouse scale and clean median-of-7 measurements, making the small point-lookup timings plausible. Independent Git verification confirms that `56179e1` contains the baseline, Condition A, and Condition B outputs together, that its TPC-C schema/workload files are byte-identical to the current inputs, and that subsequent loader changes only configure the database connection. The -2.206 percentage-point B benefit relative to A can therefore be reported as a high-confidence provenance result, with the Q12 outlier noted as an interpretation caveat.

JOB's Condition B result is complete: 113 rows, 14 finalized indexes, 82 tool calls, and no missing execution times. The clean redo summary reports a genuinely reset database, a consistent session/cache state, and median-of-7 measurements. Baseline/A/B were updated together in commit `93e004e`; the full-file scan found no `rows=0` plus `cost=0.00..0.00` empty-result pattern. A improved 76/113 queries and B improved 49/113, but B's mean is dominated by query 28a (694343.972 ms baseline). Removing that query leaves B negative at -978.113%, so the direction is not explained solely by that outlier, although the aggregate magnitude is highly sensitive to it.

These comparisons should be read with the data caveats in `open_questions.md`: JOB is included but medium-confidence because its aggregate is outlier-sensitive, historical timeout policy is not uniformly encoded, and Git chronology cannot by itself prove identical database snapshots for every benchmark.
